    A guide dog is wonderful. Almost nobody has one.

    A guide dog stops at curbs, leads around trouble and refuses to step out in front of a car. Kite takes those same jobs and puts them in a pair of AirPods, for everyone still waiting for a dog or walking with only a cane.

    Kite is for the walk to the coffee shop, the pharmacy and the bus stop, alone, today.

    Three ways of seeing.
    One voice.

    Indoors, the chest camera alone is enough: it beeps for the door frame and reads you the menu. Outdoors, the drone adds the look-ahead a cane can never give you.

    The drone

    LOOK-AHEAD

    • Flies about 30 cm above and behind your head.
    • Sees the crowd at the corner, the crossing and the car turning in, half a block out.

    Without it: you learn the street one cane-length at a time.

    The chest camera

    REFLEXES

    • Watches the ground at your feet, which the drone can't see.
    • Point it at a menu or a sign and say "read this".

    Without it: the closest metre, where collisions happen, goes unwatched.

    The AirPods

    ONE VOICE

    • Beeps from where each thing is, faster as it gets closer.
    • Press once and talk. Any stereo Bluetooth headphones work.

    Without them: two cameras and nobody telling you anything.

    Kite is built from four parts. Take one out and it stops.

    These aren't integrations bolted onto a demo. Each one is an organ: Kite's voice, its judgment, its nervous system and its memory.

    THE VOICE AND EARS

    Cartesia

    Every word you hear and every word you say. "Stop." plays instantly from a cached voice; answers stream in about a tenth of a second.

    THE JUDGMENT

    Claude

    Turns what the cameras see into one calm sentence, reads you the menu, and makes the careful calls, but can never tell you to go.

    THE NERVOUS SYSTEM

    SpacetimeDB

    One live world shared by the drone, the earpiece and your caregiver's map, who can send you a message mid-walk.

    THE MEMORY

    Moss

    Your places and your hazards, found in milliseconds every time you speak: "my usual coffee shop", "the curb here is tall".
