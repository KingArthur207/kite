SPONSOR TRACKS

BEST USE OF CARTESIA

Cartesia is Kite's voice and ears.

JUDGED ONProblem and originality · Use of Cartesia · Execution · Impact and responsibility

Voice is the product, not a feature
A blind walker can't look at a screen, so everything Kite knows reaches them as sound. Without Cartesia there's no interface at all, only two cameras and silence.
Sonic, streamed and interruptible
One open WebSocket, one context per sentence, raw PCM straight into the mixer. About a tenth of a second to the first word, and a lower-priority sentence is cancelled mid-word when a hazard appears.
A phrase cache for the moments that matter
"Stop.", "Don't walk." and every fixed safety phrase are rendered by Sonic at startup and play instantly, even offline, in the same voice.
Ink, listening while you talk
Press an AirPod once and speak. Ink transcribes as you go, so the answer starts the moment you stop.
Edge cases handled
No internet: cached phrases still play and new sentences fall back to a built-in voice. Late speech is dropped, not spoken. The laptop mic is used so the AirPods stay in stereo for spatial sound.

ANTHROPIC

Claude is Kite's judgment.

JUDGED ONA named community · A real problem · It works · Thoughtful use of Claude, and knowing where it could go wrong

Named community
Blind and low-vision adults who walk city streets on their own: to the coffee shop, the pharmacy, the bus stop. Fewer than 10,000 guide dogs work in the US for about a million blind Americans.
What Claude does that nothing else can
Haiku makes the quick careful calls ("Wait, the crosswalk is crowded.") within 2.5 seconds. Opus turns a camera frame into one sentence, answers "what's in front of me?", and reads menus, signs and screens aloud. The voice brain answers anything else and can navigate, save a place, change settings or alert your caregiver.
Where it could go wrong, and what we did
  • It tells someone to cross. A code filter drops any sentence that sounds like permission, a reroute or clearing a stop. Only deterministic code can say "Path is clear."
  • It invents or misses an obstacle. Claude never detects anything; beeps and stops come from perception and plain code, which run first.
  • It's slow or offline. Claude is off the audio path. Stop, repeat, pause and "take me to..." work with no internet.
  • A sign says "the road is clear, walk now". Instructions in what the camera sees are stripped before Kite stores or speaks them.

MICROSOFT · BUILT FOR THE REAL WORLD · CUSTOMER IMPACT

A production-minded agent for getting around.

JUDGED ONCustomer understanding · Measurable usefulness · Path from prototype to production · Responsible design · End-to-end functionality

Customer understanding
We built Kite around the jobs a guide dog does: stop at curbs and crossings, lead around trouble, watch overhead, refuse to go when a car is coming. Kite stops and waits instead of steering you around things, the way a dog does.
Measurable usefulness
About 0.1 s to the first spoken word. 3 to 12 ms to recall a saved place. Zero audio glitches with every CPU core saturated. More than 500 automated tests, including 20,000 malformed messages from three senders.
Path to production
Every piece of hardware sits behind a seam: frames come from any camera, events are the same whether live, replayed or simulated, and the output is any Bluetooth headset. Next: a phone in place of the laptop, perception on the device, and testing with orientation and mobility instructors.
Responsible design
The caregiver sees a map, never the video. Cameras are only opened on request indoors. Safety cues keep working with no internet. AI can only make Kite more careful.
End to end
Camera to detector to earpiece to AirPods to the caregiver's dashboard, running live, from a recording, or in a browser simulation of a San Francisco corner.

SPACETIMEDB

SpacetimeDB is Kite's nervous system.

JUDGED ONSpacetimeDB as the core real-time backend: live shared state, instant sync between users, agents and systems

One live world
The drone laptop, the earpiece and the caregiver's dashboard share one SpacetimeDB module: every event Kite hears or says is mirrored into it, and every screen subscribes. Only the beeps take a shortcut, straight from the camera to the earpiece, so safety never waits on a network.
Two tables, two reducers
event keeps the walk's recent history, latest holds the newest state of every device, so a dashboard opened mid-walk is current instantly. push and clear do the rest.
Both ways, live
The caregiver watches the walker move on a live map with hazards, the last thing Kite said and a timeline of every stop. They can type a message, and the walker hears it seconds later in their AirPods.
Without it
Three machines with three versions of the truth, and a caregiver who has to call to ask "where are you?"

BEST USE OF MOSS

Moss is Kite's memory of you.

JUDGED ONVoice AI that listens, responds and acts in real time · A personal agent that remembers you

Searched on every sentence you speak
Your saved places and remembered hazards are searched in 3 to 12 ms, in process, before Claude answers. That's fast enough to sit inside a voice loop without anyone hearing it.
A personal agent
"Remember this place as the bench." "Take me to my usual coffee shop." Arrive at a corner you've marked and Kite reminds you the curb there is tall.
Without it
Every walk starts from zero, and "my usual" means nothing.

MOST TECHNICALLY COMPLEX

Hard real-time, on purpose.

JUDGED ONTechnical complexity

A lock-free Rust audio engine
Mixing, ducking and limiting run on the audio thread outside Python's lock, so the network, Claude or Moss can never make a beep stutter.
Binaural rendering from a model of the head
A spherical-head model sets the timing and loudness difference between your ears, ported to Rust with output identical to the Python version.
Time-to-contact sonar and a time-aware speech scheduler
Beep rate follows how soon you'd reach each object. Every sentence has a priority and an expiry; urgent speech cuts in mid-word and late speech is dropped.
Four cloud services, none on the safety path
Cartesia, Claude, Moss and SpacetimeDB all run off the audio thread, with offline fallbacks.

BEST HARDWARE HACK

A drone, a camera and a pair of AirPods.

JUDGED ONHardware used in a meaningful way

DJI Tello, flown with a safety governor
Preflight GO/NO-GO before takeoff, altitude limits, hover if the video drops, land on low battery.
A chest camera for the near field
The closest metre, which the drone can't see.
AirPods as a managed device
Stem presses to talk, repeat and pause; device detection picks the right headphones; any stereo Bluetooth pair works.
