# Hear the street *before you reach it.*

a guide dog that flies

For blind and low-vision people who walk city streets on their own

A cane finds the pole when you hit it. Kite tells you first. A small drone flies just above and behind your head, a camera on your chest watches your feet, and your AirPods beep from where things are, faster as they get closer. A calm voice says "Stop." before you reach trouble.

## Three ways of seeing. *One voice.*

The drone looks ahead, the chest camera covers the last metre, and the AirPods turn both into sound. Indoors, the chest camera works on its own.

### The drone

look-ahead

- Flies about **30 cm above and behind** your head.
- Sees the crowd at the corner, the crossing and the car turning in, half a block out.

Without it: you learn the street one cane-length at a time.

### The chest camera

reflexes

- Watches the ground at your feet, which the drone can't see.
- Point it at a menu or a sign and say "read this".

Without it: the closest metre, where collisions happen, goes unwatched.

### The AirPods

one voice

- Beeps from where each thing is, faster as it gets closer.
- Press once and talk. Any stereo Bluetooth headphones work.

Without them: two cameras and nobody telling you anything.

## Kite is built from four parts. Take one out and it stops.

Each one does a job Kite can't do without: its voice, its judgment, its nervous system and its memory.

the voice and ears

### Cartesia

Every word you hear and say. "Stop." plays instantly from a cache; answers start in about a tenth of a second.

the judgment

### Claude

Turns what the cameras see into one calm sentence and reads you the menu. It can add caution, never tell you to go.

the nervous system

### SpacetimeDB

One live world for the drone, the earpiece and the live map.

the memory

### Memory

Your places and hazards, found in milliseconds each time you speak: "my usual coffee shop", "the curb here is tall".
